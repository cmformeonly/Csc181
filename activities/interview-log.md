## **INTERVIEW LOG**

### **Interview 1**

**Role:** Caretaker  
 **Date:** October 23, 2026  
 **Method:** In-person interview

#### **Questions and Answers**

**1\. Q: Can you share how you record repair requests from boarders?**  
 A: *“Akong isulat ang reklamo ug ang ipapalit... dayon sa magsunod \[nga caretaker\] sa akoa, akong ibilin sa iyaha...”* (I write down the complaint and what needs to be bought, then I leave it for the caretaker who comes after me.) When someone asks for a room to be fixed, I list that room. The next caretaker also writes “fixed” and when it was fixed. Madam asks whether things are already fixed, and that is how we list complaints.

**2\. Q: Has a note you left ever gone missing?**  
 A: Yes, once. I left it for another caretaker, and she said she never saw it.

**3\. Q: I previously asked for a repair, then someone else’s complaint that had waited longer came in, and you told me to wait longer and handled theirs first. What happened?**  
 A: I didn’t know about the other complaint because it was reported to someone else. I expected yours was already okay because the part had been bought. Madam scolded me when she asked whether it was already fixed. I had assumed the repairman had already been told. After some time it slipped my mind. *“Kung wala ka ninaog adto ga expect kung natauran naka.”* (If you hadn’t come down to complain, I would have assumed you were already attended to.)

**4\. Q: What happened to the siblings’ ceiling fan before they went on vacation?**  
 A: Before they left, they told Jean their ceiling fan was broken, and they were told it would be fixed when they got back. When they returned from vacation, it was still not fixed. I didn’t know about it, because I can only follow up on the complaints made to me. Jean was supposed to report it to me and update me. When I confronted Jean, she said nothing.

**5\. Q: If a boarder complains to Jean, does it reach you?**  
 A: No, I don’t know about it. What matters to me is knowing that a part has been bought, because then she automatically has it installed. I list what is broken and who complained to me. *“Kung kinsa tong niduol sa ako, mao ra gyud akong mabaw an.”* (The only complaints I know about are from the people who come to me.) So I didn’t know something else was also broken. I wonder why Jean didn’t include it in the list, since she was the one who knew.

#### **What We Learned**

* A complaint made to one caretaker may never reach the other caretaker.  
* Notes left between shifts can go missing, and the caretakers dispute whether a note was ever seen.  
* Buying the part was treated as if the repair was done, even though it had not been installed yet.  
* A complaint can slip a caretaker’s mind after some time, and she may only remember when the boarder complains again.  
* The owner (Madam) asks about the status of repairs and scolds the caretaker when a bought part has not been installed.

#### **How It Changed Our Proposal**

* The caretaker view will be one shared list of complaints per room that every caretaker sees, so a complaint does not depend on which caretaker received it or on a paper note.  
* We will add a separate status for “Parts purchased, waiting for installation,” so buying a part is not mistaken for a finished repair.  
* Each complaint will keep its date, so a caretaker can see which one is oldest without relying on memory.

### **Interview 2**

**Role:** Caretaker  
 **Date:** October 24, 2026  
 **Method:** In-person interview

#### **Questions and Answers**

**1\. Q: How do you record it when a boarder complains that something is broken?**  
 A: I write down their room number and then make the request to the owner.

**2\. Q: Have several boarders complained at the same time, and did you ever forget one?**  
 A: Yes, it has happened. They followed up and I told them my boss had not acted on it yet. I did not forget, but my boss did. *“Wala ko nakalimot pero akong amo nakalimot... nalista nako pero akong amo makalimot ba unya dugay pa makabalik kay naay lakaw.”* (I didn’t forget, my boss did. I had it listed, but she forgets, and it takes a long time for her to come back because she is out running errands.)

**3\. Q: What usually breaks?**  
 A: Ceiling fans.

**4\. Q: If a boarder complains to you and the next shift also has complaints, how do you combine them?**  
 A: The other caretaker leaves her list with me, and I keep the full list. When a complaint comes in, we hand it over so the parts can be bought directly.

**5\. Q: Who buys the parts?**  
 A: Uncle Teteng buys lights and faucets, and Madam buys ceiling fans.

**6\. Q: Who decides the repair schedule?**  
 A: It depends on whether our regular repairman (*suki*) is available. *“Kami moy magbuot magset lang sha ug schedule kung kanus a \[sha available\] pero dili gyud ingon nga dugayon niya.”* (We decide the schedule when he is available, but it is never really long.) The longest he takes to arrive is about two days.

**7\. Q: Someone posted that repairs in our boarding house took up to two weeks. What do you say to that?**  
 A: It depends on the follow-up. I also have to tell the boss, *“Kinahanglan musulti jud ko kay masuko biya pud na si madam ‘nganong karon paman ka nagsulti’.”* (I have to tell her, because Madam gets angry and asks why I only told her now.) The problem is when the person who was told about a complaint takes a long time to tell the owner.

**8\. Q: Has a note or list left by another caretaker ever gone missing?**  
 A: It should be placed where it can be seen. *“Dapat ibutang gyud niya dira. Pabal on pud ko niya dili kay mag note note rapud siya.”* (She should put it there, and she should also tell me, not just leave notes.)

**9\. Q: Who decides what to buy?**  
 A: I do. *“Akoy muingon. Ako pud mucontact sa amo.”* (I decide, and I also contact the boss.)

#### **What We Learned**

* Delays can start before any repair work: the complaint has to reach the owner, who approves and buys the parts, and she may be out on errands.  
* The caretakers set the repair date themselves with their regular repairman, and he usually arrives within about two days.  
* Longer waits depend on how quickly the person who received the complaint passes it on.  
* Avoid using loose paper for shift notes. Leave them in a visible location and communicate the details in person to prevent information loss.

#### **How It Changed Our Proposal**

* The repair date and time agreed between the caretaker and the repairman will be entered by the caretaker and shown to the boarder as the estimated repair date and time.  
* The repairman will not use the app. The app will only store his contact details so the caretaker can call or text him, and the caretaker will enter the agreed schedule.  
* Complaints will be recorded in the app for all caretakers to see, so a caretaker no longer has to rely on a note from the other shift.

### **Interview 3**

**Role:** Tenant  
 **Date:** October 25, 2026  
 **Method:** Online survey

#### **Questions and Answers**

**1\. Q: Can you walk me through your past experiences the last time you had to report broken amenities or needed fixtures like a sink, faucet, lighting, doorknob, or vending machine?**  
 A: As far as I can remember, I have only experienced a broken sink and a broken light, and both happened just recently.

**2\. Q: How did the caretaker or landowner respond when you told them your complaint?**  
 A: The landowner addressed the sink immediately. The non-functioning light took longer, and we had to follow up several times.

**3\. Q: From the day you reported it to the caretaker, how many hours or days did it take before the repairman showed up and started repairing?**  
 A: The sink was repaired right after it was reported. It didn’t take a day. The light took one week to be fixed, so we had to follow up many times, partly because the caretaker was unavailable.

**4\. Q: Between the time you reported the issue and when it was completely repaired, how did you get updates, or did it feel forgotten so that you had to follow up with the caretaker repeatedly?**  
 A: We did not receive any updates, so we had to ask for updates ourselves.

**5\. Q: How did the broken amenity affect your daily routine, studies, or sleep while you waited, and did you ever have to find a workaround or pay for the fix yourself?**  
 A: The sink did not affect me much because it was fixed quickly. The light affected my studies. It was very hard to study in my room with a light that was not working well, so I used a lamp as an alternative and even studied in a different place with proper light.

**6\. Q: If you could report and track repairs through a website or app, what would you want it to show you, and what would make you stop using it?**  
 A: I would want the app to let me report problems easily, check the status of my repair request anytime, and receive updates on when the repair would be completed. I would probably stop using the app if I still had to keep following up with the caretaker myself, or if it didn’t speed up the repair process.

#### **What We Learned**

* The sink was fixed immediately, while the light took one week and needed many follow-ups.  
* The tenant received no updates and had to ask for them.  
* The caretaker’s unavailability added to the delay.  
* A broken light affected the tenant’s studies, so they used a lamp and studied elsewhere.  
* The tenant would stop using the app if they still had to follow up with the caretaker herself or if it did not speed up repairs.

#### **How It Changed Our Proposal**

* This confirmed that tenants need to check the status of their report anytime and see when the repair will be completed, without following up in person.

### **Interview 4**

**Role:** Tenant  
 **Date:** October 25,2025  
 **Method:** Online survey 

#### **Questions and Answers**

**1\. Q: Can you walk me through your past experiences the last time you had to report broken amenities or needed fixtures like a sink, faucet, lighting, doorknob, or vending machine?**  
 A: There was a time when the fan in our room broke, so we told the caretaker downstairs. It took a few more days to be fixed because they had to buy a fan, but they fixed it themselves. *“Naay time nga naguba ang fan sa among room... mga pila pa ka adlaw ba to naayo kay nagpalit pag fan pero Ila ra biyang giayo.”*

**2\. Q: How did the caretaker or landowner respond when you told them your complaint?**  
 A: They said they would fix it, but they still had to buy a fan, so it might only be fixed the next day. They told us to just wait. *“Tagad lang daw mi.”* (They told us to just wait.)

**3\. Q: From the day you reported it to the caretaker, how many hours or days did it take before the repairman showed up and started repairing?**  
 A: I think it took 1/2 days before they came to our room and fixed it.

**4\. Q: Between the time you reported the issue and when it was completely repaired, how did you get updates, or did it feel forgotten so that you had to follow up with the caretaker repeatedly?**  
 A: I was embarrassed, so I did not go back to ask. Luckily, they remembered that my fan was broken. *“Maygani nahinumdoman nila nga nagub-an ko.”* (Luckily they remembered that mine was broken.)

#### **What We Learned**

* A broken ceiling fan took a few days to fix because a replacement fan had to be bought first.  
* The tenant was told to just wait and was not given a date.  
* The tenant did not follow up because she was embarrassed, so the repair depended on the caretakers remembering on their own.  
* The repair took about 1/2 days after the report.

#### **How It Changed Our Proposal**

* Because tenants can be embarrassed to follow up for updates, we kept reporting through the app and the dynamic status tracker. Tenants can check progress without having to ask the caretaker in person.  
* We added an update on materials bought to the status, so tenants can see when parts have been purchased while they wait for the repair.
